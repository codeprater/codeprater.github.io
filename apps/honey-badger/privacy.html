Honey Badger: The Last Hive · Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 25, 2026

Overview

Honey Badger: The Last Hive (including Savage Edition) does not collect, sell or share any personal information. There are no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking.

Information stored on your device

Your game progress, settings, and your badger's look and name are saved on your device. If you use iCloud, they sync between your own Apple devices through Apple's iCloud key-value storage. That data lives in your iCloud account and the developer cannot see it. You can erase it with Settings > Reset Progress in the game, or by deleting the app.

TrueDepth face controls, storage and sharing

In iOS version 1.1 build 10, optional face controls use Apple ARKit on your device. With camera permission, the game reads jaw opening and forward movement, blinks, gaze direction, head position, tilt and movement. The game uses these signals to control Hudson and detect stillness. Boot Camp uses them to adjust control sensitivity. Mirror mode and the camera bubble can show a live preview.

Live readings and the five Boot Camp calibration values stay in device memory. The app discards calibration when the app process ends. On launch, this build removes the five calibration preferences saved by earlier versions. The game does not write facial measurements or camera frames to files, backups or iCloud. Built-in gameplay recording hides the camera preview and disables camera and microphone capture.

The app does not send face images, facial measurements or calibration to PraterApps, other players, advertisers, analytics providers or other third parties. Nearby players exchange game actions and match state. Face data is used only for play and calibration, never for identification, analytics or advertising. Turn on Quiet Mode to use touch or controller input. You can revoke camera permission in device Settings.

Earlier versions may retain five control calibration numbers in local app preferences and may include a visible camera preview in a replay when recording is enabled. Installing and opening build 10 clears them. Previously saved or shared replay copies remain under the control of whoever saved or received them.

Photos and replay clips

If a grown-up turns on replay clips (off by default, behind a parental gate), iOS's ReplayKit records gameplay video on your device so it can be shared. Saving a stat card to Photos asks for permission to add to your photo library. Nothing is uploaded by the game. Sharing only happens when a grown-up chooses to share through the share sheet, which is also behind a parental gate.

Playing with friends nearby

Battle a Friend and Savage Edition's Nearby Face-Off use Apple's peer-to-peer connection over Bluetooth and local Wi-Fi to find players in the same room. The badger name you enter is shown to the nearby player. Only button presses, moves and match state pass between the two devices. Nothing goes to the internet or a server, and nothing is kept after a match. Your device asks for local network permission the first time.

Game Center

This version does not use Game Center for online play or global leaderboards. Your records stay on your device.

Narrator audio

The narrator uses your device's built-in voices and text. The game does not use the microphone, record or listen to you.

Children

The game does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children under 13. Anything that leaves the app (sharing, saving to Photos, turning on video recording) requires a parent or guardian to pass a parental gate.

Third parties

The game includes no third-party SDKs, ad networks or analytics services.

Changes

If this policy changes, the updated version will be posted on this page with a new date.
